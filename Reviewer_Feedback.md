COMMENTS FOR THE AUTHOR:

Reviewer #1: Thank you for the opportunity to review this manuscript. The authors conducted a cost-effectiveness analysis evaluating the addition of bevacizumab to first-line (1L) chemotherapy in patients with metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC) and chromosomal instability (CIN) across three European countries (Germany, Ireland, and Spain). A three-state Markov model with a lifetime horizon was developed using data from the ANGIOPREDICT consortium. The findings indicated that the addition of bevacizumab to standard chemotherapy was not cost-effective in any of the three countries assessed. Deterministic sensitivity analyses identified key parameters influencing the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), including the hazard ratio for bevacizumab plus standard therapy versus standard therapy alone, the utility value associated with the first-line treatment health state, and the unit cost of bevacizumab.

To me, the model is sound and appropriate, and the writing of the manuscript is of very high quality. I only have minor suggestions for the authors to improve the clarity of the manuscript.

\* The section "2. Description of Patients and Treatment Regimens" feels out of place and should be a subheading in "3. Materials and Methods"

\* The "Model Structure" subsection begins with a justification as to why the three countries were chosen. Perhaps the authors would like to consider whether some of this text is better suited in the Introduction. Description of the COLOSSUS project can remain where it is.

\* Can the authors also include the BIC for their goodness-of-fit in the supplementary?

\* I suggest that the authors revise the paragraph describing the results of the deterministic sensitivity analysis, as the current phrasing is somewhat cumbersome and difficult to understand. For example, instead of "In Figure 2, we evaluated the relative weight of each parameter individually in our model on the overall uncertainty of cost-effectiveness in a one-way sensitivity analysis," a clearer and more concise sentence could be: "Figure 2 presents the results of a one-way sensitivity analysis evaluating the individual impact of each model parameter on the overall uncertainty in cost-effectiveness outcomes."

Reviewer #2: This is an interesting and novel paper, and I congratulate the authors for addressing this topic. However, several aspects could be improved to enhance both the clarity of presentation and the robustness of the analysis, especially given that the model used is relatively simple.

Major Comments

1\. Access to data and relation to previous study

In the Methods section, it is not clear whether the authors had access to individual patient-level data from the published trial or relied solely on the publication. It is also unclear whether the authors were directly involved in the previous study. Stronger justification is needed for why this specific study was selected and whether it is the only available source informing bevacizumab efficacy.

2\. Model structure

The model is a very simple 3-state approach, but the rationale for choosing a Markov model rather than a partitioned survival model is not discussed. A stronger justification for the structural choice is needed.

3\. Utility values

The use of trial-based utilities is problematic. Trial participants often have better health status than typical patients, and the reported value (0.85) appears unrealistically high for metastatic cancer-higher than average population utilities in the UK. Moreover, the cited value from a systematic review upon checking align with colorectal cancer survivors, not patients with metastatic disease. The authors should calculate country-specific multipliers based on general population norms (e.g., NHS QOL Dashboard for England).

4\. Measurement of utilities

The methods section does not describe how utilities were measured (e.g., EQ-5D). The approach should be clarified, and multipliers applied to population reference values for each country.

5\. Deterministic vs. probabilistic analysis

The base-case results are presented deterministically. Unless explicitly justified by local guidance, probabilistic analysis should be considered the base case. Results should include net monetary benefit (NMB) and credible intervals (CrI).

6\. Survival analysis (Weibull fit)

Weibull was not clearly the best fit according to AIC or visual inspection. Alternative parametric fits should be considered at least in scenario analyses.

7\. Use of country-specific data

The limited variability in cost-effectiveness results stems from the fact that no country-specific data (other than costs) were incorporated. This limitation should be explicitly discussed.

Minor Comments

\* Ensure abbreviations are consistent: some are spelled out more than once (e.g., CIN), while others are introduced inconsistently (hazard ratio).

\* The statement that cost-effectiveness analyses cannot be generalisable contradicts existing transferability frameworks.

\* In the description of trials, please report the number of patients in intervention and comparator arms, not only tumour biopsies or samples. Clarify whether repeat biopsies are counted as separate samples.

\* CHEERS is a reporting standard, clarify how it has been used

\* The ranges used in scenario analyses should be presented and justified.

\* Formatting of Table 1 should be improved for readability (especially in PDF conversion).

\* Numbers in Table 2 do not appear to match; if this is due to rounding, please clarify. The title should state explicitly whether results are deterministic or probabilistic. For PSA, please report NMB and CrI.

Reviewer #3: Thank you for giving me the opportunity to review this work which investigated the cost effectiveness of bevacizumab for metastatic Colorectal Cancer with Chromosomal Instability.

The manuscript is well written, is clear, concise with good reader flow. In the main, the analyses follow standard processes and thus the outputs are informative and useful.

\* The only substantial comment that I have pertains to the parameter model choice for KM data extrapolation. The authors provide a 2014 citation to support the statement 'Weibull models have been demonstrated to be more suitable for modelling events occurring in mCRC'. The primary aim of the cited work was not to determine the most suitable parameter models. Further, there are numerous other CEAs published (and, in particular, in the 10 years since) in this disease state that have concluded that other models are more appropriate. As standard, extrapolation models should be chosen based on AIC/BIC criteria, visual fit and clinical plausibility. I see that AIC (along with visual fit and clinical plausibility) were considered here. I would advise that BIC is also evaluated. I note also, that the AIC indicated that Weibull was not necessarily the best fit for progression; the gamma distribution may be more appropriate here.

Of note, I do not see that parameter choice has been investigated in the deterministic SA. This is an omission. There is much evidence to suggest that CEAs in cancer are often sensitive to the choice of curve.

I would suggest that the decisions regarding the choice of curve and the investigations of the impact of uncertainty are revisited.

\* There is a lack of transparency regarding the choice of utilities. Values of 0.85 (for the first-line health state) and 0.65 (for the second-line health state) have been derived from Ramsey et al. These values (particularly 0.85) lack some face validity.

On review of Ramsey et al, I see that the authors used two different tools and also investigated utilities according to stage & time at diagnosis. It is not clear how the authors here extracted data from Ramsey et al.

I do not see that Ramsey et al determined that, average HUI scores (measured 12 months prior), were 0.85 for survivors versus 0.65 for those who died (n=10). It may be that these values were extracted by the authors here. I note that the health states do not appear to be aligned with those of the model.

it would be useful to understand why these values are considered appropriate and, in particular, why they are more appropriate that other values available in the literature.

I have a number of other small comments:

\* It would be useful if the jurisdiction(s) were mentioned in the title.

\* In the abstract, the authors note that 'adding bevacizumab was only cost-effective when willingness to pay (WTP) thresholds were multiple times larger than currently accepted cut-offs'. Suggest that this is reworded to state that bevacizumab is not cost effective even at thresholds that are appreciably higher than those that are considered realistic (or similar wording).

\* The argument that by choosing these three countries, the authors have captured the diversity of health expenditure across the EU is not compelling. Spain sits only below the EU average. The public health investment is a number of eastern European countries is much lower. I would suggest that this argument be reworded to be more valid.

\* The authors inform us that Smeet et al concluded that 'Bevacizumab significantly improved progression-free survival (HR = 0.68; 95% CI 0.53--0.87) and overall survival (HR = 0.65; 95% CI 0.49−0.86)'. It would be useful for the reader to also understand what the differences in median PFS and OS versus SoC were.

\* The authors state that 'bevacizumab may be the most successful of all targeted therapies'. It is problematic that this statement is made whilst being supported by an outdated (2006) citation.

\* The authors state that a 5-and-a half-year time horizon was considered appropriate to estimate life expectancy in this patient population. It is necessary to inform the reader if the extrapolations indicated that this time horizon was appropriate (i.e. 100% of patients had experienced the event of interest in the extrapolated curves). The extrapolated curves, presented in the supplemental appendix, indicate that this time horizon was appropriate, but it would be useful to state this within the body of the manuscript.

\* The transition probability of second-line treatment into death is as informed by a 2016 citation in a different population. It would be useful to know why a relatively old citation was used here. It may be due to a paucity of more recent data perhaps?

Likewise, the Tree trial was published in 2008.

\* Details are provided, in the appendix, about the parameter variations used in the deterministic SA. I would consider that a brief description should be provided within the main body of the manuscript.

\* The tornado plots are very busy and thus difficult to decipher. Perhaps the top 10 parameters only could be presented (per country) and the x-axis range reduced. Please add a footnote to define all abbreviated text. It would be useful to categorically state that the standard threshold were not met in any of these investigations.

\* Table 1: The sources for all inputs should be provided. I see that there are no citations, for example, for any Irish drug cost data. It may be that assumptions had to be made in some cases. These should be provided and justified.

The presentation of the application of discounts rates, in table 1, is not clear.

\* In the discussion, the authors state that 'We hypothesised that this improved efficacy may consequently increase efficiency, such that bevacizumab may finally reach a reasonable cost-per-QALY threshold to be deemed cost-effective'. I expect that 'efficiency' here should be replaced with 'value'.

\* I have may missed the detail, but I do not see how the PSA was undertaken. For example, was this MC PSA and how many iterations were undertaken.

I have some very small editorial comments:

\* The authors state that 'threshold of €78,871 is applied as suggested by a recent assessment of the German'. The outcome (i.e. QALY) here is missing.

\* The terms 'phase 3' and 'phase III' are used interchangeably. In the interests of consistency, the preferred should be used throughout.

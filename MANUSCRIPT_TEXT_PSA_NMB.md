## Methods Section Addition

### Insert after the existing PSA description (after line 971):

From the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, we calculated incremental costs, incremental QALYs, and net monetary benefit (NMB) for each of the 10,000 simulations. Net monetary benefit was computed as:

NMB = (Incremental QALYs × WTP threshold) − Incremental cost

where incremental outcomes represent the difference between the experimental strategy (bevacizumab added to chemotherapy) and standard of care (chemotherapy alone). NMB provides a single metric that converts health benefits into monetary terms, facilitating probabilistic decision-making. A positive NMB indicates the experimental strategy provides value for money at the given willingness-to-pay threshold.

To quantify uncertainty in cost-effectiveness outcomes under probabilistic assumptions, we summarized the distributions of incremental costs, incremental QALYs, and NMB using means and 95% credible intervals (CrIs). Credible intervals were calculated as the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the simulated distributions, representing the range containing 95% of simulated values given our parameter uncertainty and chosen distributions.

## Results Section Addition

### Insert after the existing PSA description (after line 608, before or after the CEAC discussion):

To quantify uncertainty in cost-effectiveness outcomes, we calculated incremental costs, incremental QALYs, and net monetary benefit from the probabilistic sensitivity analysis. Table X presents the mean and 95% credible intervals (CrI) for these outcomes across all three countries. Mean incremental costs ranged from €30,847 (Ireland) to €41,699 (Germany), with credible intervals indicating substantial uncertainty in these estimates. Mean incremental QALYs were similar across countries (0.171-0.173 QALYs), reflecting the common effectiveness data used for all analyses.

Net monetary benefit at country-specific willingness-to-pay thresholds was negative in all countries, with mean NMB ranging from €-23,181 in Ireland to €-27,529 in Spain. The 95% credible intervals for NMB in all countries excluded zero and remained substantially negative, confirming that the addition of bevacizumab does not represent value for money even when accounting for parameter uncertainty. These probabilistic results corroborate the deterministic findings and demonstrate that conclusions regarding cost-effectiveness are robust to joint parameter uncertainty.
